2. Paper structure  
Use these headings and stay within ±10% of each word count.  
Section  Words  What to write  
Title  ≤ 15  Method + problem + context, not just the topic 
area  
Abstract  200–250 Problem, gap, method, evaluation, main result 
with a number, significance  
Index Terms  4–6 terms  Specific keywords, alphabetical  
I. Introduction  600–800 Lead the reader from the broad area to your 
specific work in four to five paragraphs.  
1. Context:  introduce the domain and why it 
matters now, with 2 –3 citations.  
2. Problem:  describe the specific difficulty that 
users, developers or researchers face.  
3. Gap:  explain what existing approaches fail to 
solve, with citations.  
4. Research questions:  state 1 –2 clearly, for 
example “RQ1: Does [your method] improve 
[metric] compared with [existing 
approach]?”  
5. Contributions:  list 3 –4 concrete 
contributions as bullet points, each one 
something the paper actually shows.  
6. Organisation:  end with one sentence on 
how the rest of the paper is organised.  
The Introduction only motivates the work. 
Detailed literature goes in Related Work, and 
technical details go in Methodology.  
II. Related Work  700–900 10–15 papers grouped by theme, critically 
compared; a comparison table of 6 –10 key 
papers on problem, method, dataset, metrics, 
key results, limitations and relevance to your 
work; your research gap  
III. Background / Problem 
Formulation  300–500 Give the reader the foundation needed to 
understand your method. Explain the key 
concepts, techniques or models your work builds 
on, briefly and only what is needed. Define the 
terms and notation used in the rest of the paper 
--- PAGE 3 ---
Section  Words  What to write  
(add a notation table if you use many symbols). 
Then state your problem formally: what the 
input is, what the output should be, the 
assumptions you make, the constraints, and 
what is out of scope.  
IV. Proposed Methodology  900–1,200  Explain your approach so that someone else 
could rebuild it. Include: an architecture figure 
showing the main components of your system or 
model and how they connect; a workflow figure 
showing the step -by-step flow from input to 
output; an algorithm in pse udocode with input, 
output and numbered steps; numbered 
equations with every symbol defined; and the 
reason for each design choice (why this method, 
model or parameter over alternatives).  
V. Experimental Setup  400–600 Describe how you tested your work so the 
results can be reproduced. State the dataset or 
test environment (source, size, how it was 
prepared or split), tools and versions used 
(language, libraries, frameworks, hardware or 
platform), the baselines you compa re against 
(existing methods or tools), and the evaluation 
metrics with a one -line reason for each. Give a 
parameter table listing every setting used, such 
as hyperparameters, configurations, number of 
runs and random seeds  
VI. Results and Analysis  800–1,000  Present results in the order of your research 
questions. Include a results table comparing 
your method with the baselines on every metric 
(best values in bold), and result figures such as 
comparison graphs, trends with a varying 
parameter, or error analysi s. Repeat 
experiments at least 3 times and report mean ± 
standard deviation. Include at least one ablation 
study (remove or change one component and 
show its effect). For every table and figure, say 
what it shows, compare with the baseline, and 
explain why  the result occurs.  
VII. Discussion  300–500 Interpret your results; do not repeat them. 
Explain what the results mean in practice and 
why your method performed better or worse in 
specific cases. State the limitations of your work 
honestly, such as small dataset, simulation 
instead of real conditions , limited scale, or cost.

Section  Words  What to write  
Discuss threats to validity: anything that could 
make your results misleading, such as bias in test 
data, possible implementation errors, or 
whether results would hold on  other data or 
settings. Mention ethical considerations, if any.  
VIII. Conclusion and Future 
Work  200–300 Answer each research question in one sentence, 
restate the main result with its number, and give 
2–3 specific future directions.  
References  15–25 entries  IEEE style, every entry cited in the text and 
verified  
3. Figures and tables  
• Figure caption goes below  the figure; table caption goes above  the table.  
• Number figures as Fig. 1, Fig. 2 … and tables as Table I, Table II …, in order of first 
mention.  
• Every figure and table must be referred to by number and explained in 2 –3 sentences, 
for example: "Fig. 3 illustrates …", "It can be observed from Table II that …", "As shown 
in Fig. 4, …". Never write "the figure below".  
• No screenshots of code, terminals, tools or web pages. Redraw diagrams (draw.io, 
TikZ) and plot graphs from your data.  
• Plots in vector format or at least 300 dpi, with labelled axes, units and legends.

before the references.
